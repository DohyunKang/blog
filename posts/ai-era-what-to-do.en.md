These days the same post shows up in dev communities all the time: **"AI writes all the code, so what are developers supposed to do now?"** The first time I read those, it stung a bit. I spent over four years studying C and CAN communication, and now it feels like people are saying "AI does that for you."

But after doing development with AI every day, my thinking has changed a little. So I'm writing about that today.

## The value of "typing" code has clearly dropped

I have to admit this. A screen that used to take a day, a script that used to take half a day, now comes out of a few lines of description. That speed gap will only get wider.

So being good at typing code won't be enough to stand out. That's not really sad, it's just **the tools changing.** Calculators came out and mathematicians didn't disappear.

## But oddly, some things matter more now

When you develop with AI, you keep hitting the same walls.

- **If you don't know what to build, AI also builds plausible garbage.** Fast, and a lot of it.
- You need **someone to judge** whether the result is right. Saying wrong things with confidence is AI's specialty.
- **Who will use it, why, and how** is something AI won't decide for you.

So the bottleneck moved from "building" to **"what, and why."** And you can't solve that well knowing only tech, or only planning. Someone who knows a bit of both seems to have the edge.

> The cheaper building gets, the more expensive the eye for choosing what to build becomes.

## But the bottleneck seems to sit even deeper than "what"

After writing this far, something felt missing. I said "what, and why", but where does that "why" come from?

If I ask AI for ideas, it gives me ten or a hundred. But picking one and **actually pushing to do it** is still up to a person. The reason I believe it's right, my own thinking about why it should be done, the strength to carry it through. That doesn't seem to come from skill. It comes from **how I want to live and what I think matters.** To put it grandly, it's values. To put it simply, it's "this looks good to me."

The other thing is the human side. However well AI talks, it feels different when a person listens to you, is happy with you, or worries about you. When someone uses what I made and says "that made things easier, thanks," that's what gives me the most energy. Helping someone else feels the same. I don't think AI can take over that part. Giving it and receiving it only really works between people.

To sum up: AI does the building. What's left for people is **thinking about why we do it, pushing it forward, and caring about other people.** That side doesn't grow just by studying, so I think I have to build it slowly by reading, meeting people, and writing posts like this.

## Where I felt the danger

Honestly, some of it scares me. One is **piling up code I don't understand.** If I keep moving on because "it runs", at some point I've built something I can't explain. From then on I'm not a developer, just someone copy-pasting.

So I made some rules for myself.

1. **Separate "it runs" from "I understand it."** If I don't understand something, I dig in until I do.
2. **Doubt what AI gives me at least once.** Ask "why did it do it this way?"
3. **Work harder on the basics** (data structures, operating systems, communication). You need to know something to judge anything.
4. **Don't only look at the tech.** Write down often why I'm doing something and who it's for. And don't skimp on time with people.

## No conclusion yet

While writing this I wanted to end with something cool like "how to survive in the AI era", but honestly I don't know yet. Half of this might be wrong when I read it again in a year.

One thing I am sure of, though. **Using it myself every day and feeling it out beats just sitting there worrying.** Anxiety grows from not knowing, and once you use it you get a sense of how far it goes and where it gets stuck.
