These days dev communities post the same thing every other day. "AI writes all the code, so what do developers do now?" At first those posts stung. I'd spent over four years on C and CAN communication, and it felt like hearing "AI does that for you."

But after developing with AI every day, my view shifted a little. So I'm writing it down today.

## The price of typing code has clearly dropped

I have to admit this. A screen that took a day, a script that took half a day, now comes from a few lines of description. The gap will keep widening.

So being good at typing code won't set anyone apart. That's not sad. The tool just changed. Calculators came out, and mathematicians didn't disappear.

## Oddly, some things matter more now

When I develop with AI, I keep hitting the same walls.

- If I don't know what to build, AI builds plausible garbage too. Fast, and a lot of it.
- Someone has to judge whether the result is right. Saying wrong things confidently is what AI does best.
- Who will use it, why, and how. AI won't decide that for me.

The bottleneck moved from "building" to "what, and why." Knowing only tech or only planning doesn't solve that well. Someone who knows a little of both seems to have the advantage.

> The cheaper building gets, the more an eye for what to build is worth.

## The bottleneck seems to sit deeper than "what"

After writing this far, something felt missing. I said "what, and why," but where does the "why" come from?

If I ask AI for ideas, it gives ten or a hundred. But picking one and pushing to actually do it is still up to a person. The reason I believe it's right, my own thinking about why it should be done, the strength to carry it through. That doesn't come from skill. It seems to come from how I want to live and what I think matters. Grandly put, values. Simply put, "this looks good to me."

The other part is between people. However well AI talks, it feels different when a person listens, is glad with me, or worries about me. When someone uses what I made and says "that made things easier," that gives me the most energy. Helping someone feels the same. I don't think AI can take over that. Giving it and getting it only works between people.

AI does the building. What's left for people is thinking about why, pushing it forward, and caring about people. Studying doesn't grow that quickly. So I'll go slowly, reading, meeting people, and writing posts like this.

## Where it feels risky

Honestly, some of this scares me. One thing is piling up code I don't understand. If I keep moving on because it runs, one day I'll have built something I can't explain. From then on I'm not a developer. I'm just copy-pasting.

So I made some rules.

1. Separate "it runs" from "I understand it." If I don't understand it, I dig until I do.
2. Doubt what AI gives me at least once. Ask "why did it do it this way?"
3. Work harder on the basics (data structures, operating systems, communication). To judge, I have to know something.
4. Don't look only at the tech. Write down often why I'm doing it and who it's for. Don't skimp on time with people.

## No conclusion yet

I wanted to write a good conclusion like "how to survive the AI era," but honestly I don't know yet. In a year, half of this may be wrong.

One thing is certain. Using it myself every day and feeling it out beats sitting around worrying. Anxiety grows from not knowing. Once you use it, you get a sense of how far it goes and where it gets stuck.

In the next post I'll write something more personal, about how I'm trying to live from here.
