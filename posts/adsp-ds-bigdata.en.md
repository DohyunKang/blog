The Data Science unit may seem like there is a lot to memorize, but its structure is simple. Just divide the competencies into hard skills and soft skills, and accurately identify which item belongs to which category.

## What is Data Science?

Data science is the work of finding valuable information from data. This unit asks what competencies a person who does this work—that is, a data scientist—must possess.

A data scientist is an expert who finds valuable information in data and proposes directions for business strategy based on that information. It is important not to describe them as someone who only deals with analysis techniques; the role also includes transforming analysis results into strategies that an organization can execute. This definition leads to the discussion of the proportion of soft skills mentioned later.

This profession has not been in the spotlight for long. In 2012, Thomas Davenport and DJ Patil wrote an article titled "Data Scientist: The Sexiest Job of the 21st Century" in *Harvard Business Review*. The article begins by stating that computer engineers and Wall Street quants were popular professions in the 1990s, but now companies are competing to hire data scientists.

## Hard Skills

Hard skills refer to technical competencies. There are four in total.

| Hard Skill Item | One-line Description |
|---|---|
| Theoretical Knowledge | Theoretical knowledge about big data |
| Techniques & Methodologies | Acquisition of relevant techniques and methodologies |
| Analysis Proficiency | Skillful handling of analysis tools and technologies |
| Design & Know-how | Accumulation of optimal analysis design and know-how |

Hard skills are described as areas that can be acquired relatively quickly through education. This means they grow by learning from books, using tools, and working on projects.

## Soft Skills

Soft skills are about turning analysis into value. Memorize them in three groups.

| Soft Skill | What it Includes |
|---|---|
| Insightful Analysis | Creative thinking, curiosity, critical logic |
| Persuasive Communication | Storytelling, visualization |
| Cross-domain Collaboration | Communication |

Connecting analysis results to actual value is considered a soft skill; therefore, when explaining data scientist competencies, the emphasis is placed on this side. Even if the results are accurate, nothing changes if practitioners do not understand them.

There is one point that frequently appears in exams here. Storytelling and visualization belong to "Persuasive Communication." Because of the word "visualization," it can easily be mistaken for a hard skill. Remember that it is classified as an ability to show something understandable to others, not merely the use of tools to draw graphs; this will help reduce confusion.

<details>
<summary>Decision sequence when confused about hard vs. soft skills</summary>

When you see a certain capability listed, ask yourself these questions:

1. Can it be learned alone through books and tools? If yes, it is a hard skill. Theory, methodologies, tool proficiency, and design know-how fall under this category.
2. Does it require people or context to be used? If yes, it is a soft skill. Curiosity, critical thinking, persuasion, and collaboration belong here.
3. If it is a soft skill, determine which group it belongs to. Ideas generation falls under insight, showing something to others falls under communication, and working with people from other fields falls under collaboration.

This sequence is merely a summary to help memorize items, but the final criterion is the classification table provided in the textbook.

## The Three Competencies Mentioned by Gartner

Gartner has summarized data scientist competencies into three areas: data management, analytical modeling, and business analytics. This classification operates on a different axis than the hard/soft skill distinction, so questions may combine or mix these categories. It is safer to remember this as a separate classification rather than trying to map it directly to the four hard skill items based solely on their names.

## Why Does the Humanities Trend Appear?

The part of this unit that asks in sentence form relates to humanistic thinking. The question is why humanities are needed for a profession dealing with data, and the answer explains it through three environmental changes:

- Globalization has shifted from simple integration toward increasing regional and cultural diversity.
- The center of business has moved from product production to customer experience as a competitive service.
- Economic logic has changed from production to creating markets.

As the world becomes more complex, customer experience becomes a source of competitiveness, and weight shifts toward creating new markets, the ability to read people and context within data has become necessary. Therefore, it follows that the ability to interpret why customers behave in certain ways, judge the context and ethical issues embedded in data, and communicate these insights persuasively has become important.

When memorizing, reduce it to three pairs of keywords: from simplicity to complexity, from product to service, from production to market creation. If the direction is reversed in a question item, the sentence is incorrect. For example, "shifted from service-centered to product-centered" would be wrong.

## Summary

- **Definition**: An expert who identifies value from data and proposes business strategy directions.
- **Hard Skills**: Theoretical knowledge, techniques/methodologies, proficiency in analysis technology, design/expertise.
- **Soft Skills**: Insightful analysis (creative thinking, curiosity, logical criticism), persuasive communication (storytelling, visualization), cross-disciplinary collaboration (communication).
- Storytelling and visualization are soft skills.
- Humanities Trend: Simple → Complex, Product → Service, Production → Market Creation.
