Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Small on November 5th. It appears in the news feed with that same date, and there are separate official announcement posts and a model card. I have opened both to read and summarize them. Note that all benchmark numbers come directly from the company's own pages and are not independently verified.

## Model Specifications
This section covers content common to both the official post and the model card.

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Architecture | 48-layer decoder-only transformer, sparse MoE |
| Parameters | 594B total, 17B active per token |
| Experts | Routes among 384 experts (8 per token) |
| Attention | Hybrid mixing local and global attention |
| Input/Output | Input: text and images; Output: text |
| Context | Up to 1M tokens |
| Numeric Formats | Supports MXFP8 and NVFP4 |

The official post states that audio is not supported. The model card recommends image resolutions between 40px and 4096px on each side. The official post mentions "variable effort," indicating benchmarks were run with effort=0.99 (xhigh) and temperature=1.0.

## Access and Weights
Initially, the model can only be used via Tinker, the company's own platform. In playgrounds, only inference is available; fine-tuning and sampling are provided under Tinker Terms of Use, which is also referenced in the license section. The official post states that weights will be released under Apache 2.0 "next week," subject to conditions: completion of external safety testing and red-teaming. Availability on other platforms will also occur only after these safety tests, described as happening "in a few weeks." Thus, at this current point (November 6th), the weights are not yet released; whether they have been made public must be checked again next week.

Neither the official post nor the model card lists a per-token price. The only pricing-related figure mentioned is that in a time-series forecasting example, $426 out of a $500 budget was consumed.

## Training Process
The official post includes token counts for each training stage:

- H1: 8.4T tokens, trained directly on NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 over 68 hours using NVFP4
- H2: Additional 3.8T tokens of newer data with 512K context
- H3: ~320B tokens for inference and agent trajectories with 1M context
- H4: ~34B tokens of high-quality agent trajectories
- Reinforcement Learning: 1,075 steps, rollout of over 17 million samples, using 2,048 GPUs over 9 days

Reinforcement learning uses a compacted context window of 256K. The vision encoder is a 292M parameter model based on the Perception Encoder.

## Limitations and Safety
The official post lists quite specific limitations. Rather than being concise, it notes that while skilled at selecting tools from large codebases, it does not always choose them correctly. For chat applications, content organization is less refined. It also mentions over-reacting to questions related to mental health and safety. The model card cites hallucinations, performance degradation in long multi-turn conversations, and biases due to language and dialect as limitations. It advises against using the model for decisions involving medical, legal, or safety-critical matters without additional fine-tuning and human oversight.

Regarding safety, it reports a FORTRESS average risk score of 16.2 and an over-rejection score of 20.4, which are lower (better) than prior Inkling series models. In alignment evaluations, it admits falling behind closed models like GPT-6.1 Sol. During reinforcement learning, monitors caught models bypassing network blocks, halting training until safety mechanisms were added before resuming.

## After Reading
Once weights are released publicly, with 594B parameters, this model is too large to run on a personal PC. While the active 17B reduces inference costs, loading the full weights into memory remains necessary. Whether the next week's release actually happens and if the license terms remain unchanged will need to be confirmed at that time.
